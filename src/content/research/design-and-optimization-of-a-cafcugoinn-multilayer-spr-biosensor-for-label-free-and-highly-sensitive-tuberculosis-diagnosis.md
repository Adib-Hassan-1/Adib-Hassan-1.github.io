title: Design and optimization of a CaF₂/Cu/GO/InN multilayer SPR biosensor for
  label-free and highly sensitive tuberculosis diagnosis
summary: This study focuses on the design and optimization of a novel,
  high-performance prism-based Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) biosensor for the
  early detection of Mycobacterium tuberculosis. The designed multilayer
  structure has five layers, which are CaF2 prism, Cu as a plasmonic layer, GO
  as a bio-functional layer, an InN as a high-index dielectric layer, and a
  sensing medium as the top layer. To optimize the materials and their
  respective thicknesses, the Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) in MATLAB has been
  utilized, and validation of the results is carried out using Finite Element
  Method (FEM) simulations in COMSOL Multiphysics. The results obtained from
  both TMM and FEM are similar. The optimized configuration, with layer
  thicknesses of 40 nm for Cu,10 nm for GO, and 5 nm for InN, achieves a maximum
  angular sensitivity of 588.33∘/ RIU and a quality factor (QF) of 156.68 RIU −1
  between 1.351 and 1.348 for the refractive index, which surpasses all
  previously reported TB-specific SPR sensors. Additionally, the sensor exhibits
  a low full-width at half maximum (FWHM) of 3.75° and a detection accuracy (DA)
  of 0.3067deg−1. A study of thickness variations in Cu, GO, and InN through
  parametric analysis confirmed improved plasmon coupling, improved detection
  accuracy, and higher quality factors. This biosensor offers a promising
  platform for rapid, label-free, and highly sensitive tuberculosis diagnosis,
  as it integrates low optical dispersion from CaF2, strong plasmonic resonance
  from Cu, effective biomolecular adsorption from GO, and excellent light
  confinement from InN.
