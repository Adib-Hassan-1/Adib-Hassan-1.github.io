title: Secure Outage Performance Analysis of Reconfigurable Intelligent
  Surface-Aided Networks with Selection Combining Diversity Scheme
summary: This paper investigates the outage-based secrecy performance of
  reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted wireless communication
  systems with selection combining (SC) diversity over Rayleigh fading channels
  in the presence of a passive multi-antenna eavesdropper. Unlike existing
  RIS-assisted secrecy studies that mainly focus on single-antenna reception
  without diversity combining, this work considers a single-antenna source
  communicating with a multi-antenna destination through an RIS, while a passive
  multi-antenna eavesdropper attempts to intercept the transmission. Since the
  direct links between the source and both the destination and the eavesdropper
  are blocked by physical obstacles, all communications are established through
  an RIS equipped with N passive reflecting elements. Exact closed-form
  analytical expressions for the probability of non-zero secrecy capacity
  (PNZSC), intercept probability (IP), and secure outage probability (SOP) are
  derived and verified through Monte Carlo simulations. Numerical results
  demonstrate that increasing the number of RIS reflecting elements N and
  legitimate receiver antennas Nd significantly improves secrecy performance,
  whereas increasing the number of eavesdropper antennas Ne degrades secrecy
  reliability. Furthermore, RIS deployment can partially compensate for stronger
  eavesdropping conditions without increasing transmit-side complexity. The
  obtained results confirm that integrating RIS technology with low-complexity
  SC diversity provides an effective and energy-efficient solution for enhancing
  physical-layer security in future wireless networks.
